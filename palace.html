KERATON SURAKARTA HADININGRAT · JAVA

The Palace

Reading the
Keraton

The compound is laid on a single axis from the mountain in the north to the sea in the south. Touch any ground on the plan to learn what happens there.

Palace plan · schematic N ↑

The Northern Square

Alun-Alun Lor

The great open ground before the palace, guarded by twin banyan trees. Every approach to the keraton begins by crossing it on foot.

Hall of Assembly

Pagelaran

An open pillared hall where officials once gathered before audience. Its columns count the ranks of the court.

The Elevated Ground

Sitihinggil Lor

Literally “high earth” — the raised platform used for coronations and the most solemn state ceremonies.

The Principal Gate

Kori Kamandungan

The threshold between the public world and the inner court. Beyond it, protocol changes entirely.

Royal Library

Sasana Pustaka

Home to manuscripts, chronicles, and genealogies — the written memory of the dynasty.

The Great Pavilion

Pendopo Sasana Sewaka

The main open hall of the keraton, where the court receives guests and holds ceremony. Open on all sides: the palace has nothing to hide from those it welcomes.

The Women’s Quarters

Kaputren

The residence of the princesses and ladies of the court, and the workshop where court batik is drawn.

The Water Garden

Taman Sari

Bathing pools and pavilions set among water — a retreat, and a lesson in stillness.

The Golden Hall

Bangsal Kencana

The gilded throne hall at the heart of the compound, reserved for the sovereign.

The Southern Square

Alun-Alun Kidul

The southern ground, facing the sea. It closes the axis that runs from mountain to ocean through the throne.

Symbols & Palace Life

The Axis

Mount Merapi to the north, the southern sea beyond. The keraton sits on the line between them, and every gate repeats that orientation.

Colour & Cloth

Certain batik patterns — parang barong among them — were reserved for the sovereign. What a person wore inside these walls stated exactly where they stood.

Abdi Dalem

The court servants who keep the compound, the ceremonies, and the gamelan. Many serve for life, and their families for generations.
