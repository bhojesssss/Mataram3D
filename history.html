History

A Kingdom
Through Time

From a granted forest to two courts. Move along the line to follow the kingdom through the moments that changed it.

1558 — 1755

1558

The Grant of Mentaok

Sultan Hadiwijaya of Pajang grants the forest of Mentaok to Ki Ageng Pemanahan as reward for defeating Arya Penangsang. What arrives is wilderness; what leaves it, a generation later, is a kingdom.

Related records →

1584

Panembahan Senopati

Danang Sutawijaya, son of Pemanahan, declares independence from Pajang and establishes his seat at Kotagede. The royal legend of his covenant with Kanjeng Ratu Kidul, queen of the southern sea, begins here — a compact between the throne and the unseen.

Related records →

1613

Sultan Agung Ascends

Sultan Agung Hanyakrakusuma takes the throne and turns Mataram into the dominant power of Java. Under him the court becomes not only a seat of government but the standard of Javanese language, etiquette, and art.

Related records →

1628

The Siege of Batavia

Twice Sultan Agung sends his armies against the VOC stronghold at Batavia. The city holds, but the campaigns mark the moment the kingdom defines itself against a foreign power on its own coast.

Related records →

1633

The Javanese Calendar

Sultan Agung merges the Hindu-Javanese Saka reckoning with the Islamic lunar calendar, creating the Anno Javanico still used for court ceremony. It is his most enduring act: a single measure of time for two inheritances.

Related records →

1645

Imogiri

Sultan Agung dies and is laid to rest at Imogiri, the burial hill he built for his line. Rulers of both Surakarta and Yogyakarta are still interred there — the one place the divided house remains whole.

Related records →

1680

Kartasura

After rebellion forces the abandonment of Plered, Amangkurat II founds a new keraton at Kartasura. The century that follows is one of contested succession and deepening Dutch involvement in court affairs.

Related records →

1745

Surakarta Hadiningrat

Pakubuwono II moves the court from ruined Kartasura to the village of Sala on the Bengawan Solo. The new keraton is laid out on the old cosmological axis — sea to the south, mountain to the north.

Related records →

1755

The Treaty of Giyanti

The treaty divides Mataram into Kasunanan Surakarta and Kasultanan Ngayogyakarta. Two courts, one inheritance: from this point the culture is carried forward in parallel, each keeping its own version of the same memory.

Related records →

Origins

Before there was
a kingdom, there
was a forest.

Mataram does not begin with a conquest. It begins with a reward — a stretch of woodland called Mentaok, handed to a loyal servant of Pajang who chose to clear it rather than trade it.

Within one generation his son had declared a kingdom there. Within three, that kingdom set the standard for Javanese language, dress, music, and time itself.
